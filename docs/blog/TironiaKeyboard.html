March 21, 2026 4 min read

Intro

Two collegues have the hobby of costumizing keyboards and one day they brought them with them. The sound it created tingled in the ear and for the first time I understood their passion. But for me, it is a hobby that is still to expensive.

Until I stumbled upon a Video from Christian Selig who explained the process of building a custom keyboard, which seemed to be simple to me. So I decided to give it a try.

Basically, it starts with a Battery which is connected to buttons which is connected to a computer. If a button is pressded it sends the coordinates like on a chess board or the game sinking the ship. The computer then translates this coordinates into a predetermend symbol.

Planing

Now that we know we want a split keyboard I started with a tool called ergogen. With a simple json-like syntax you are able to create a grid ressembling your keyboard. The posibilities are endless you can twist and turn them, but only in two dimensions. And because I wanted a more ergonomic design, I went with this. Of course the depends strongly on the wished layout which defines the number of keys.

in Ergogen

The rule of ergonomics states that your finger should not be moving further than a key away from its resting position or “home” row. And because my preferences were different from the creators online. My design started to look much more different than in the turorial. This leant that later, I had problems, that the creators did not have.

Layout design

The next step is to import what we did into an actual pcb design tool. I used Kicad because all of the tutorials and videos recommended it and already had step by step instructions. Even if it looked slightly different i found it easy to use and navigate.

One of the first versions, with the components still missing and the controller on the back. The outline not defined yet.

Tironia and Stenography

Stenography is a method of rapid writing using vowel sounds instead of individual letters. To achieve such speeds, multiple keys are pressed simultaneously. Professional stenographers can reach speeds of 200-300 words per minute which is basically the speed of talking. Which is why this method is used in court reporting and live captioning. It will one day probably be replaced by Audio recognition or similar. But for me this is a dream and a goal to reach nearly 3x speed of my current personal best of 139 wpm.

similar video that brought me the idea => exceeding my average of 90 words per minute on a normal keyboard.

Looking at the video, I felt that this Keyboard was not that different from the one in the tutroial and it felt possible to create my own.

Assembly & Solder

From here on, I started to learn more than any tutorial or school project could have teached me and is in my view the most valueable part of this project.
I have never soldered before. And I have never worked with such small components. So after watching a few videos and reading some guides, I felt ready to give it a try.

Starting was a good approach but What I lacked was controll.

The big Brackets over the hole make it possible to swap switches.

If you looked closely at the picture you might have noticed that some brackets were soldered the opposite way, blocking the hole. When I soldered those, I thought it did not matter. Big mistake. if the brackets are in the wrong place the switches (buttons) will not fit which makes the keyboard unusable.

Now, I could have just desoldered them, but this was not the only flaw.

the white square is where you insert the usb-c cable

There is no space. I can not physically fit the usb-c cable there. The compoments are in the way, there is no way to fit that there.

Firmware / ZMK

The more interesting part. I have never worked with Keyboard firmware before. For Tironia I have decided to use ZMK which supports wireless keyboards and was one of the only optinos if i did not want to code the firmware from zero.

I ran into several problems, like bluetooth not working, linking the pins of the microcontroller to the right keys, and the general configuration of the firmware that I sometimes had to just brute force until it worked. This took me longer than expected because zmk looked different each time I worked on this project. Few months ago, it got a major overhaul and the documentation bring information for those who wanted to customize their Layout. I got it to connect via bluetooth and right now I am working on the registration of the keys.

qerftwrfttgtrtrwstsrstrebbbqaaaikololololololololololololololioikikniknikikikkkkujujujujjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjbuububuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuo
Worst case is that this is a hardware problem I have to order new parts ans solder them on one of my spare pcbs. Why do I think this is a hardware problem?

This is a single diode, not bigger than half a cm. in length and barely 3mm in width

flow of current through a diode

As you can see it in the picture: This mm thin component has a specific direction for the current to flow which is marked by just barerly visible lines and a W1 as you can see in the top picture. To identify it you need special alot of light. I reckon if it is in the wrong direction, the current can not flow and the key will be stuck on pressed.

btw: No tutorial told me about this, I had to research possible problems myself, until I found out multiple months have passed.
